\chapter{Dynamic local detuning for Rydberg MWIS encodings}
\label{chap:mwis-dynamic-detuning}

This chapter asks a deliberately narrow question: can programmable local
detunings on the atoms already present in a crossing-with-edge (CWE) encoding
replace an encoding-induced exponentially small gap by a polynomial gap?  The
answer is yes for the elementary instance consisting of two logical vertices
and one edge, but with two important qualifications.  First, time dependence
does not enlarge the instantaneous linear space of realizable diagonal
operators.  In fact, the exact degree-balancing potential proposed in
Ref.~\cite{bombieri2025quantum} is not realizable on the unmodified CWE by
detuning its existing atoms, whether those detunings are static or dynamic.
Second, a different, reachable control is sufficient: a two-atom dynamic
``corridor catalyst'' removes the geometrical corner state from the relevant
spectral window and changes the endpoint-sweep gap from exponential to
$\Theta(L^{-2})$.

The polynomial statement proved below is a theorem for the second-order
domain-wall Hamiltonian in the blockade limit.  Its transfer to a finite-
blockade, finite-$\Omega/\Delta$ experiment requires a separate leakage and
model-error check; those conditions are stated explicitly rather than hidden
in the effective-model result.  The final sections turn the CWE calculation
into a general control-space and weighted-Poincar\'e framework.

\section{MWIS cost function and Rydberg realization}
\label{sec:mwis-cost-rydberg}

Let $G=(V,E)$ be a graph with positive vertex weights $w_v$.  For binary
variables $n_v\in\{0,1\}$, consider
\begin{equation}
 H_{\mathrm{MWIS}}(\mathbf n)
 =-\sum_{v\in V}w_v n_v
 +\sum_{(u,v)\in E}U_{uv}n_un_v .
 \label{eq:mwis-cost}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Penalty encoding of MWIS]
\label{lem:mwis-penalty}
If $U_{uv}>\max\{w_u,w_v\}$ for every edge $(u,v)$, every minimizer of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-cost} is an independent set, and its occupied vertices form
a maximum-weight independent set.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose a minimizing configuration contains an occupied edge $(u,v)$.  Set
$n_v$ to zero.  The energy change is
\begin{equation}
 H_{\mathrm{MWIS}}(n_v=0)-H_{\mathrm{MWIS}}(n_v=1)
 =w_v-\sum_{a:\,(a,v)\in E}U_{av}n_a<0,
\end{equation}
because the sum contains at least $U_{uv}>w_v$.  This contradicts
minimality.  On the independent-set subspace the penalty term vanishes, so
minimizing Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-cost} is equivalent to maximizing
$\sum_vw_vn_v$.
\end{proof}

The driven Rydberg Hamiltonian is taken to be
\begin{equation}
 H_{\mathrm{Ryd}}(s)
 =\Omega(s)\sum_{a}\sigma_a^x
 -\sum_a[\Delta_a^{(0)}(s)+u_a(s)]n_a
 +\sum_{a<b}U_{ab}n_an_b,
 \qquad s\in[0,1].
 \label{eq:mwis-rydberg}
\end{equation}
Here $u_a(s)$ is a programmable local shift of the detuning.  In the ideal
blockade model, configurations containing two excited atoms within the
blockade radius are projected out.  The remaining classical configurations
are independent sets of the physical unit-disk graph.  Vertex wires and
crossing gadgets extend this native geometry to arbitrary logical
connectivity~\cite{nguyen2023quantum}; local light shifts provide the relevant
experimental control primitive~\cite{oliveira2025demonstration}.

For an annealing path, the instantaneous and minimum gaps are
\begin{equation}
 \delta E(s)=E_1(s)-E_0(s),\qquad
 \delta E_m(L)=\min_{s\in[0,1]}\delta E(s).
 \label{eq:mwis-gap-definition}
\end{equation}
Changing only the speed $s(t)$ does not change Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-gap-definition};
changing $u_a(s)$ deforms the Hamiltonian path itself.  Gap-aware scheduling
and path optimization should therefore be distinguished~\cite{zeng2016schedule,jeong2026enhanced}.

\section{The CWE low-energy manifold}
\label{sec:mwis-cwe-manifold}

\subsection{Physical gadget and configuration-space geometry}

The CWE joins two even vertex wires $x$ and $y$.  Denote by $m_w$ the last
ordinary atom before the gadget on wire $w$.  Its central five-atom block is a
wheel graph: $G_c$ is the hub and
$(G_x,G_y,m_x+1,m_y+1)$ form the four-cycle.  The incoming atoms $m_x$ and
$m_y$ attach to $G_x$ and $G_y$, respectively.  This observation gives a
useful consistency check: the five-atom blockade Hilbert space has physical
dimension eight, as in the tensor-network construction of
Ref.~\cite{bombieri2025quantum}.

For the two-wire setup, define
\begin{equation}
 L_w=\frac{N_w}{2}+2,
 \qquad
 \zeta_w=\frac{m_w}{2}+1 .
 \label{eq:mwis-length-zeta}
\end{equation}
The low-energy basis states are labeled by the locations of the two domain
walls, $\ket{\mathbf j}_d=\ket{j_x,j_y}_d$.  The CWE constraint removes the
states in which both domain walls have passed the gadget.  Thus
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma_L=
 \left\{(j_x,j_y):
 1\leq j_w\leq L_w,\qquad
 \neg(j_x>\zeta_x\ \text{and}\ j_y>\zeta_y)
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:mwis-L-domain}
\end{equation}
It is useful to split this L-shaped configuration graph into
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
 \mathcal I&=\{j_x\leq\zeta_x,\ j_y\leq\zeta_y\},
 &\text{(inner region)},\\
 \mathcal X&=\{j_x>\zeta_x,\ j_y\leq\zeta_y\},
 &\text{(horizontal lead)},\\
 \mathcal Y&=\{j_x\leq\zeta_x,\ j_y>\zeta_y\},
 &\text{(vertical lead)}.
\end{align}
\label{eq:mwis-three-regions}
\end{subequations}
Nearest-neighbor changes of one domain wall give the Manhattan edges of
$\Gamma_L$.  The CWE adds one diagonal hopping edge,
\begin{equation}
 (\zeta_x+1,\zeta_y)\longleftrightarrow
 (\zeta_x,\zeta_y+1).
 \label{eq:mwis-gadget-diagonal-edge}
\end{equation}
For example, $N_x=N_y=8$ and $m_x=m_y=4$ give
$L_x=L_y=6$, $\zeta_x=\zeta_y=3$, and
$6^2-3^2=27$ domain-wall states.  Their exchange graph has 42 Manhattan
edges plus the edge in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-gadget-diagonal-edge}.  These integer
invariants are useful for detecting an incorrectly transcribed gadget.

\subsection{Schrieffer--Wolff projection}

Set the common bulk and gadget detuning to $\Delta>0$ and write
\begin{equation}
 H_0=-\Delta\sum_a n_a,\qquad
 V=H_\Omega+H_u,\qquad
 H_\Omega=\Omega\sum_a\sigma_a^x,\qquad
 H_u=-\sum_a u_a n_a .
 \label{eq:mwis-H0-V}
\end{equation}
Let $P$ project onto the one-domain-wall-per-wire manifold and
$Q=\mathbb I-P$.  To second order,
\begin{equation}
 H_{\mathrm{eff}}
 =PH_0P+PVP
 +PVQ\frac{1}{E_0-QH_0Q}QVP
 +\mathcal R_{\mathrm{SW}} .
 \label{eq:mwis-SW}
\end{equation}
This is the standard degenerate Schrieffer--Wolff expression
\cite{bravyi2011schrieffer}.  A single spin flip leaves the manifold; a second
flip can exchange an excitation with its neighbor and move a domain wall by
one lattice step.  The resulting hopping is
\begin{equation}
 t=\frac{\Omega^2}{\Delta}.
 \label{eq:mwis-hopping}
\end{equation}
The first-order local-detuning contribution is diagonal.  If
\begin{equation}
 M_{\mathbf j,a}={}_d\!\bra{\mathbf j}n_a
 \ket{\mathbf j}_d\in\{0,1\},
 \label{eq:mwis-occupation-map}
\end{equation}
then, up to a scalar energy shift,
\begin{equation}
 H_{\mathrm{eff}}(s)
 =-t(s)A_{\Gamma_L}
 -\operatorname{diag}\!\left[M\mathbf u(s)
 +\boldsymbol\epsilon_{\mathrm{end}}(s)\right]
 +\mathcal R_{\mathrm{SW}}(s).
 \label{eq:mwis-effective-general}
\end{equation}
The matrix $A_{\Gamma_L}$ includes Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-gadget-diagonal-edge}.
For local controls and $|u_a|,\Omega\ll\Delta$, a typical matrix element of
the omitted term is of order
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(\frac{\Omega^3}{\Delta^2}
 +\frac{\Omega^2\|\mathbf u\|_\infty}{\Delta^2}
 +\frac{\|\mathbf u\|_\infty^2}{\Delta}\right),
 \label{eq:mwis-SW-remainder-scale}
\end{equation}
with geometry-dependent combinatorial factors.  Equation
\eqref{eq:mwis-SW-remainder-scale} is an order estimate, not yet a uniform
many-body norm bound.

For a single ordinary wire with $N=2(L-1)$ atoms, adjacent domain-wall states
differ by moving one excitation across a pair:
\begin{equation}
 F(j+1)-F(j)=u_{2j-1}-u_{2j},
 \qquad
 F(j)=\sum_a u_a n_a(j).
 \label{eq:mwis-wire-control-difference}
\end{equation}
Consequently, individually addressed detunings can synthesize any desired
one-dimensional potential $F(j)$ up to an additive constant.  This elementary
identity will be reused below.

\section{Why the endpoint-only protocol has an exponential gap}
\label{sec:mwis-localization-gap}

At the midpoint of the logical protocol, the endpoint detunings vanish and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-effective-general} reduces to $-tA_{\Gamma_L}$.  For fixed
lead widths $\zeta_x,\zeta_y=O(1)$ and $L\rightarrow\infty$, its continuum
limit is the Helmholtz problem on an L-shaped waveguide with Dirichlet
boundaries.  A corner of such a waveguide supports an eigenvalue below the
lowest transverse threshold~\cite{dauge2012quantum}.  In the discrete problem,
write
\begin{equation}
 E_{\perp,x}=-2t-2t\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{\zeta_y+1}\right),
 \quad
 E_{\perp,y}=-2t-2t\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{\zeta_x+1}\right).
 \label{eq:mwis-strip-thresholds}
\end{equation}
If $E_{\mathrm{loc}}<\min(E_{\perp,x},E_{\perp,y})$, the lead amplitudes
decay exponentially,
\begin{equation}
 |\psi_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbf j)|
 \leq C e^{-\mu d(\mathbf j,\mathcal I)} .
 \label{eq:mwis-agmon-decay}
\end{equation}
This is the graph analogue of waveguide evanescence and can be formalized by
an Agmon metric~\cite{steinerberger2022agmon}.

Suppose the desired logical state is $\ket{10}_l$, at the far end of
$\mathcal X$.  An endpoint detuning $w$ creates a second localized branch
$\ket{\psi_{\mathrm{tar}}(w)}$ near $j_x=L_x$.  At the value $w_*$ where the
two diabatic energies coincide, their separation is $O(L_x)$ and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-agmon-decay} implies
\begin{equation}
 \left|\matrixel{\psi_{\mathrm{loc}}}{H_{\mathrm{eff}}}
 {\psi_{\mathrm{tar}}}\right|
 \leq C' e^{-\mu' L_x}.
 \label{eq:mwis-exp-matrix-element}
\end{equation}
The two-level reduction at the crossing is
\begin{equation}
 H_{2\mathrm{lev}}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 E_{\mathrm{loc}}&V_L\\
 V_L^*&E_{\mathrm{tar}}
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \delta E(w_*)=2|V_L|=O(e^{-\mu' L_x}).
 \label{eq:mwis-two-level-crossing}
\end{equation}
This is encoding-induced hardness: the logical instance is only one weighted
edge, while the physical configuration-space geometry creates the tunneling
distance.  It also explains why an $O(1)$ separation between the corner bound
state and the continuum is compatible with an exponentially small annealing
gap: these are two different gaps.

An exponentially small instantaneous gap need not always imply an
exponential optimal runtime.  Some paths can traverse two small crossings
diabatically and return to the desired branch, as occurs for the unconstrained
crossing gadget~\cite{bombieri2025quantum}.  For the hard CWE endpoint sweep,
there is only one relevant branch exchange, so a purely diabatic traversal
ends on the wrong branch.  Both $\delta E_m$ and real-time success probability
must therefore be checked; related deliberately diabatic improvements for
hard Rydberg instances are discussed in Ref.~\cite{schiffer2024circumventing}.

\section{What local detuning can and cannot synthesize}
\label{sec:mwis-control-space}

\subsection{The occupation-map column space}

The central atom occupied in a CWE domain-wall configuration records which
region of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-three-regions} contains the effective particle:
\begin{equation}
 n_{G_c}=P_{\mathcal I},\qquad
 n_{G_x}=P_{\mathcal X},\qquad
 n_{G_y}=P_{\mathcal Y},\qquad
 n_{G_c}+n_{G_x}+n_{G_y}=\mathbb I_{\Gamma_L}.
 \label{eq:mwis-gadget-region-projectors}
\end{equation}
Here a number operator denotes its restriction to the domain-wall manifold.
Every ordinary atom on wire $x$ depends only on $j_x$, and similarly for
$y$.  Moreover, on the allowed L-domain,
\begin{equation}
 P_{\mathcal I}
 =\mathbb I-\mathbf 1_{j_x>\zeta_x}-\mathbf 1_{j_y>\zeta_y}.
 \label{eq:mwis-inner-separable}
\end{equation}
Together with Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-wire-control-difference}, this proves the
following statement.

\begin{lemma}[Instantaneous diagonal controllability]
\label{lem:mwis-separable-control}
In the ideal CWE domain-wall manifold, arbitrary local detunings on all
existing atoms generate, modulo a scalar shift, exactly the separable
configuration-space potentials
\begin{equation}
 (M\mathbf u)(j_x,j_y)=f_x(j_x)+f_y(j_y).
 \label{eq:mwis-separable-potential}
\end{equation}
At any fixed $s$, replacing static detunings by dynamic detunings changes the
coefficients in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-separable-potential}, but not this linear
space.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The preceding occupation identities show that every column of $M$ is a
function of $j_x$ alone, a function of $j_y$ alone, or their sum plus a
constant.  This proves containment in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-separable-potential}.
Conversely, Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-wire-control-difference} independently fixes
all adjacent differences of $f_x$ and $f_y$; the gadget transition is supplied
by Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-gadget-region-projectors}.  Additive constants are
physically irrelevant.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A no-go result for exact degree balancing}

Let $d(\mathbf j)$ be the degree of a site in $\Gamma_L$, including the CWE
diagonal edge.  The degree-balancing prescription is
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon_{\mathrm{bal}}(\mathbf j)=t[4-d(\mathbf j)].
 \label{eq:mwis-degree-balance}
\end{equation}
With the sign convention of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-effective-general}, it gives
\begin{equation}
 -tA_{\Gamma_L}-\operatorname{diag}\epsilon_{\mathrm{bal}}
 =-4t\mathbb I+t\mathcal L_{\Gamma_L},
 \label{eq:mwis-laplacian-form}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal L=D-A$ is the combinatorial graph Laplacian.  The ground state
is uniform and its gap is $t\lambda_2(\mathcal L_{\Gamma_L})$, which is
$\Theta(tL^{-2})$ for fixed-width connected leads.  Ref.~\cite{bombieri2025quantum}
adds ancillary atoms so that Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-degree-balance} can also be
imposed on the nonlogical boundary.

The same potential cannot be synthesized by detuning only the original CWE
atoms.  Any separable function obeys the vanishing mixed-difference identity
\begin{equation}
 f(a,c)+f(b,d)-f(a,d)-f(b,c)=0.
 \label{eq:mwis-mixed-difference}
\end{equation}
Assume $\zeta\geq3$ and $L\geq\zeta+3$ in the symmetric geometry.  The four
allowed sites
\begin{equation}
 (1,1),\quad(\zeta,1),\quad(1,\zeta+2),\quad
 (\zeta,\zeta+2)
\end{equation}
have $4-d$ values $2,1,1,1$, respectively.  Their mixed difference is one,
contradicting Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-mixed-difference}.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon_{\mathrm{bal}}\notin
 \operatorname{Col}(M)+\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}.
 \label{eq:mwis-rank-obstruction}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:mwis-rank-obstruction} is a useful negative result.  A
dynamic waveform cannot repair an instantaneous rank obstruction.  Its real
advantage is instead to realize a different reachable path, to track the
hopping scale $t(s)$, and to vanish at the endpoints so that the encoded cost
function is unchanged.

\section{A reachable dynamic corridor catalyst}
\label{sec:mwis-corridor-catalyst}

Take the unique target to be $\ket{10}_l$, so the relevant corridor is
$\mathcal X$.  Let
\begin{equation}
 P_{\mathrm{in},x}
 =\sum_{j_y=1}^{\zeta_y}
 \proj{\zeta_x+1,j_y}
 \label{eq:mwis-entrance-projector}
\end{equation}
be its entrance cross section.  Direct inspection of the wheel gadget gives
the two exact occupation identities
\begin{equation}
 P_{\mathcal X}=n_{G_x},\qquad
 P_{\mathrm{in},x}=n_{G_x}-n_{m_x+1}.
 \label{eq:mwis-corridor-identities}
\end{equation}
Thus the configuration-space catalyst
\begin{equation}
 V_{\mathrm{cat}}(s)
 =-\Lambda(s)P_{\mathcal X}+t(s)P_{\mathrm{in},x}
 \label{eq:mwis-catalyst}
\end{equation}
is implemented by only two existing physical atoms:
\begin{equation}
 u_{G_x}(s)=\Lambda(s)-t(s),\qquad
 u_{m_x+1}(s)=t(s),
 \label{eq:mwis-two-atom-controls}
\end{equation}
because $H_u=-\sum_a u_an_a$.  For a target in $\mathcal Y$, exchange
$x\leftrightarrow y$.

Choose a smooth envelope $g(s)$ supported in the ordered, gap-limiting part of
the protocol and set
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda(s)=\kappa t(s),\qquad
 u_{G_x}(s)=g(s)(\kappa-1)t(s),\qquad
 u_{m_x+1}(s)=g(s)t(s),
 \quad g(0)=g(1)=0.
 \label{eq:mwis-dynamic-waveform}
\end{equation}
The controls are $O(t)=O(\Omega^2/\Delta)$ and hence remain parametrically
smaller than $\Delta$ in the perturbative regime.  The first term in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-catalyst} lowers the entire target lead, rather than a
single distant endpoint.  The second term is not cosmetic: it compensates the
extra attraction induced by coupling the entrance to two different parts of
the CWE junction and enables a simple positive-semidefinite certificate.

\subsection{No-bound-state certificate}

For a transparent theorem, take $\zeta_x=\zeta_y=c=O(1)$ and
$L_x=L_y=L$.  Let $P_F$ project onto the final cross section $j_x=L$, and
consider the active-window Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
 H_L(w)=-tA_{\Gamma_L}-wP_F
 -\kappa tP_{\mathcal X}+tP_{\mathrm{in},x},
 \qquad 0\leq w\leq w_f .
 \label{eq:mwis-active-H}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Removal of the geometrical bound state]
\label{prop:mwis-no-bound-state}
Let
\begin{equation}
 c_0=\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{c+1}\right),\qquad
 \kappa>3-2c_0.
 \label{eq:mwis-kappa-condition}
\end{equation}
At $w=0$, $H_L(0)$ has no eigenvalue below the bottom of the shifted target-
strip band,
\begin{equation}
 E_*=-\kappa t-2t(1+c_0).
 \label{eq:mwis-shifted-threshold}
\end{equation}
The statement remains valid for semi-infinite leads, so it is not a
finite-size observation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Partition $\Gamma_L=\mathcal X\cup\mathcal R$, with
$\mathcal R=\mathcal I\cup\mathcal Y$.  The target lead is the Cartesian
strip $P_{L-c}\mathbin{\square}P_c$.  After subtracting $E_*$, its internal
quadratic form is
\begin{equation}
 t(2I-A_{P_{L-c}})\otimes I
 +tI\otimes(2c_0I-A_{P_c})
 +tP_{\mathrm{in},x}.
 \label{eq:mwis-strip-positive-form}
\end{equation}
The second summand is positive semidefinite because the largest eigenvalue of
$A_{P_c}$ is $2c_0$.  The first summand is the one-dimensional Dirichlet form;
in addition to squared finite differences, it contains one factor
$t|\psi|^2$ on every entrance site.  The explicit entrance term supplies a
second such factor.

Every entrance site has one edge into $\mathcal R$, except
$(c+1,c)$, which also has the diagonal CWE edge and therefore has two.  For
each cross edge $(a,b)$ use
\begin{equation}
 -t(\overline\psi_a\psi_b+\overline\psi_b\psi_a)
 +t|\psi_a|^2+t|\psi_b|^2
 =t|\psi_a-\psi_b|^2\geq0.
 \label{eq:mwis-edge-square}
\end{equation}
The two entrance factors in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-strip-positive-form} pay for
all cross edges on the $\mathcal X$ side.  Each site in $\mathcal R$ is the
endpoint of at most one such edge.  Since the maximum degree of its induced
graph is at most four,
\begin{equation}
 -tA_{\mathcal R}-E_*I-tD_{\mathrm{cross}}
 \succeq t(\kappa-3+2c_0)I\succ0.
 \label{eq:mwis-R-positive}
\end{equation}
The full form $H_L(0)-E_*I$ is therefore a sum of positive semidefinite terms.
This excludes a corner eigenvalue below $E_*$ for finite or infinite leads.
\end{proof}

For $c=3$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-kappa-condition} requires only
$\kappa>3-\sqrt{2}\simeq1.586$.  The numerical example below uses
$\kappa=2$.

\subsection{Polynomial gap during the endpoint sweep}

\begin{theorem}[Effective-model gap conversion]
\label{thm:mwis-polynomial-gap}
Fix the lead width $c$, choose $\kappa$ strictly above
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-kappa-condition}, and take $w_f>t$.  Apart from an intended
logical degeneracy, if present, there are constants $a_c,b_c>0$ independent of
$L$ such that, for sufficiently large $L$,
\begin{equation}
 a_c\frac{t}{L^2}
 \leq
 \min_{0\leq w\leq w_f}
 [E_1(H_L(w))-E_0(H_L(w))]
 \leq b_c\frac{t}{L^2}.
 \label{eq:mwis-poly-gap-bound}
\end{equation}
In contrast, setting $\kappa=0$ and removing the entrance term gives the
endpoint-only avoided crossing in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-two-level-crossing}, with
gap $O(e^{-\mu L})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:mwis-no-bound-state} places the entire non-target region
a finite energy above the target strip threshold.  Eliminating that region by
a Schur complement produces a finite-width, finite-range boundary condition
at the entrance of $\mathcal X$.  The strict inequality in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-kappa-condition} makes the corresponding threshold
resolvent regular: it cannot generate a subthreshold pole or an exponentially
localized competing branch.

The remaining longitudinal problem is a finite Jacobi chain of length
$\ell=L-c$ with an $L$-independent Robin condition at its entrance and an
endpoint potential $-w$ at its far end.  Its extended levels obey
\begin{equation}
 k_n\ell+\delta_{\mathrm{in}}(k_n)
 +\delta_F(k_n,w)=n\pi,
 \label{eq:mwis-phase-quantization}
\end{equation}
where the two scattering phases and their first derivatives are bounded near
$k=0$.  Hence $k_1=\Theta(L^{-1})$,
$k_2-k_1=\Theta(L^{-1})$, and the quadratic dispersion
$E(k)=E_*+tk^2+O(k^4)$ gives a $\Theta(tL^{-2})$ level spacing.

The only change of spectral character occurs when the far-end potential
creates its desired boundary state.  For an isolated semi-infinite hopping
chain, this happens at $w=t$; for $w>t$ the decay ratio and longitudinal energy
are
\begin{equation}
 r=\frac{t}{w},\qquad
 E_{\mathrm{end}}=-w-\frac{t^2}{w}.
 \label{eq:mwis-end-bound-state}
\end{equation}
At the threshold the finite-size gap is again $\Theta(tL^{-2})$, whereas a
fixed distance above threshold gives an $O(1)$ binding gap.  The regular
entrance boundary shifts the threshold and constants but not the power of
$L$.  Compactness of the closed interval $0\leq w\leq w_f$ then gives the
uniform lower bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-poly-gap-bound}; a trial state made
from the first two longitudinal strip modes gives the upper bound.  The final
statement follows from Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mwis-agmon-decay}--\eqref{eq:mwis-two-level-crossing}.
\end{proof}

The theorem addresses the gap-limiting endpoint sweep.  A complete logical
protocol can activate Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-dynamic-waveform} while the state is
within the $O(c)$-sized junction region, release it into the selected corridor,
ramp the target endpoint through Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-end-bound-state}, reduce
$\Omega$ to zero while the final MWIS bias keeps the target unique, and only
then turn off $g(s)$.  The finite-junction stages have $L$-independent gaps;
the release and endpoint-binding thresholds have $\Theta(tL^{-2})$ gaps.  At
$\Omega=0$ the last removal is diagonal and does not alter the final MWIS
ordering.  Thus the catalyst need not remain in the problem Hamiltonian.

\section{Numerical check of the analytic mechanism}
\label{sec:mwis-numerics}

Figure~\ref{fig:mwis-dynamic-gap} diagonalizes
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-active-H} with $t=1$, $c=3$, and a target endpoint sweep
$0\leq w\leq3t$.  The endpoint-only data fit
$\delta E_m\propto e^{-0.351L}$ over the displayed sizes.  With
$\kappa=2$ and the entrance term $tP_{\mathrm{in},x}$, a finite-size fit gives
$L^{-2.21}$ and approaches the analytic $L^{-2}$ law.  At the baseline
avoided crossing, the wavefunction has separated junction and endpoint peaks
with an exponentially small valley.  The controlled wavefunction instead
occupies the target corridor, so endpoint binding is a one-dimensional
threshold phenomenon rather than tunneling between distant localized states.

 \centering

\section{In what sense dynamic detuning is better}
\label{sec:mwis-benefits}

The comparison must specify its baseline.

\paragraph{Versus endpoint-only encoding.}
The improvement is strict within the effective model:
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-two-level-crossing} gives an exponentially small gap,
whereas Theorem~\ref{thm:mwis-polynomial-gap} gives $\Theta(tL^{-2})$.
The control acts on the cause of the gap---a localized configuration-space
branch---rather than merely slowing the schedule near its crossing.

\paragraph{Versus static degree balancing with ancillary atoms.}
Both methods recover a polynomial gap.  Exact Laplacian balancing is more
solution-agnostic and gives a uniform ground state, but it modifies the gadget
and increases atom/connectivity overhead.  The corridor catalyst uses two
existing atoms and leaves the final cost Hamiltonian unchanged.  It is not
universally superior: it uses a selected corridor and requires time-resolved
local addressability.  Equation~\eqref{eq:mwis-rank-obstruction} also shows
that it is not a hidden implementation of the ancilla strategy.

\paragraph{Why time dependence matters.}
A static term $-\Lambda P_{\mathcal X}$ would change the encoded weights at the
end of the anneal.  The envelope $g(s)$ provides three benefits:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item the catalyst is present only in the ordered localization window and is
 absent from both endpoint Hamiltonians;
 \item its scale follows $t(s)=\Omega(s)^2/\Delta(s)$, so the dimensionless
 no-bound-state condition remains fixed as the Rabi drive changes;
 \item the catalyst can remain active while the endpoint bias selects and
 binds the target, and can be removed after hopping is switched off.
\end{enumerate}
These are path-design benefits, not an increase of the rank of $M$.

\paragraph{Why a diagonal catalyst is not automatically beneficial.}
Diagonal catalysts can introduce new local minima or favor a state far from
the solution~\cite{albash2021diagonal}.  The present construction avoids that
guesswork because its sign, support, and sufficient amplitude follow from a
spectral partition of the configuration graph.  For a larger problem, an
analogous certificate or a full-path numerical validation is necessary.

\section{Returning to the full Rydberg Hamiltonian}
\label{sec:mwis-full-model-transfer}

Let $\Delta_{\mathrm{eff}}$ be the certified effective gap and suppose the
domain-wall band is separated from leakage states by $\Delta_{\mathrm{leak}}$.
If an operator-norm estimate gives
\begin{equation}
 \|\mathcal R_{\mathrm{SW}}(s)\|\leq\eta(s),
 \qquad
 2\eta(s)<\Delta_{\mathrm{eff}}(s),
 \label{eq:mwis-transfer-condition}
\end{equation}
Weyl's inequalities imply
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{\mathrm{full}}(s)
 \geq\Delta_{\mathrm{eff}}(s)-2\eta(s).
 \label{eq:mwis-weyl-transfer}
\end{equation}
The same projection also requires
$\Delta_{\mathrm{leak}}\gg\Delta_{\mathrm{eff}},\|\dot H\|^{1/2}$ on the
timescale of interest.  A crude use of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-transfer-condition}
can become demanding because the desired gap scales as $L^{-2}$.  Structured
errors that preserve a corridor or a weighted-Laplacian form are much less
harmful than an arbitrary perturbation of the same norm, so full-model spectra
and dynamics should supplement the worst-case bound.

Finite van der Waals tails add a configuration-dependent diagonal term
$v_{\mathrm{tail}}(\mathbf j)$.  In the linear approximation, it can be
included in the same reachability equation,
\begin{equation}
 M\mathbf u(s)\simeq
 \mathbf b(s)-\mathbf v_{\mathrm{tail}}(s),
 \label{eq:mwis-tail-compensation}
\end{equation}
subject to hardware amplitude and slew limits.  Local-detuning compensation
of such inhomogeneities is compatible with the experimental control used in
Ref.~\cite{oliveira2025demonstration}, but calibration errors and light-shift
crosstalk must be measured for a quantitative claim.

Finally, spectral improvement is not by itself an algorithmic benchmark.  A
full validation should report the many-body instantaneous gap, leakage from
the domain-wall band, and
\begin{equation}
 P_{\mathrm{sol}}(T)=
 |\braket{\psi_{\mathrm{MWIS}}}{\psi(T)}|^2
 \label{eq:mwis-success-probability}
\end{equation}
as a function of runtime.  This separates genuinely harmful avoided
crossings from gaps that can be traversed diabatically.

\section{A general framework for dynamic detuning}
\label{sec:mwis-general-framework}

The CWE calculation suggests a reusable workflow for an arbitrary encoded
optimization problem.

\subsection{Projection and reachability}

Choose a low-energy configuration basis $\{\ket{\alpha}\}$ and derive
\begin{equation}
 H_{\mathrm{eff}}(s,\mathbf u)
 =K(s)-\operatorname{diag}[\mathbf v_0(s)+M(s)\mathbf u(s)]
 +\mathcal R(s).
 \label{eq:mwis-general-effective}
\end{equation}
The columns of $M$ are the projected number operators, augmented by the
appropriate Schrieffer--Wolff response if controls also change hopping.  Since
a scalar potential is irrelevant, define the centered projector
$C=I-|\mathcal C|^{-1}\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^T$ and test
\begin{equation}
 C\mathbf b(s)\in\operatorname{Col}[CM(s)].
 \label{eq:mwis-general-reachability}
\end{equation}
If exact reachability fails, solve the constrained residual problem
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf u_*(s)=
 \arg\min_{\mathbf u}
 \|W(s)[M(s)\mathbf u-\mathbf b(s)]\|_2^2
 \label{eq:mwis-general-ls}
\end{equation}
with endpoint, amplitude, bandwidth, and slew-rate constraints included from
the beginning.  Dynamic control changes which reachable vector is used at
each $s$; it does not invalidate Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-general-reachability}.

\subsection{Designing an extended ground state}

Uniform degree balancing is one member of a larger family.  Select a strictly
positive target profile $\boldsymbol\phi(s)$.  The condition
$H_{\mathrm{eff}}\boldsymbol\phi=E\boldsymbol\phi$ requires the diagonal
vector
\begin{equation}
 b_\alpha^{\phi}(s)
 =\frac{[K(s)\boldsymbol\phi(s)]_\alpha}{\phi_\alpha(s)}-E(s).
 \label{eq:mwis-positive-profile}
\end{equation}
If $\mathbf b^\phi-\mathbf v_0$ is reachable, Perron--Frobenius positivity
makes $\boldsymbol\phi$ the unique ground state of a connected stoquastic
configuration graph.  More importantly, the ground-state transform gives the
exact Dirichlet form
\begin{equation}
 \matrixel{\psi}{H_{\mathrm{eff}}-E}{\psi}
 =\frac12\sum_{\alpha,\beta}
 t_{\alpha\beta}\phi_\alpha\phi_\beta
 \left|
 \frac{\psi_\alpha}{\phi_\alpha}
 -\frac{\psi_\beta}{\phi_\beta}
 \right|^2,
 \label{eq:mwis-ground-state-transform}
\end{equation}
where $K_{\alpha\beta}=-t_{\alpha\beta}\leq0$.  The spectral gap is therefore
a weighted Poincar\'e constant.  If the ratios of $\phi_\alpha$ and the edge
weights are polynomially bounded and the weighted configuration graph has
polynomial conductance, Eq.~\eqref{eq:mwis-ground-state-transform} certifies a
polynomial gap without requiring a uniform wavefunction.

If only an approximate potential is reachable, let
$\mathbf r=\mathbf b^\phi-\mathbf v_0-M\mathbf u_*$.  A general but
pessimistic Weyl estimate is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(\mathbf u_*)
 \geq\Delta_\phi-2\|\mathbf r\|_\infty.
 \label{eq:mwis-residual-weyl}
\end{equation}
For $\Delta_\phi=\Theta(tL^{-z})$, this demands a residual of the same small
order.  The CWE proof illustrates a better alternative: exploit the support
and sign of the residual to prove that no eigenvalue lies below a lead
threshold.  Localization is a geometric property, so a geometry-aware
quadratic-form certificate can be much sharper than a norm bound.

\subsection{Path optimization after the analytic reduction}

Once a safe low-dimensional control basis is known, one may optimize the path
rather than all atom waveforms blindly.  For a nondegenerate gap and control
operator $B_a=\partial H_{\mathrm{eff}}/\partial u_a$, the
Feynman--Hellmann gradient is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial\delta E}{\partial u_a}
 =\matrixel{1}{B_a}{1}-\matrixel{0}{B_a}{0}.
 \label{eq:mwis-gap-gradient}
\end{equation}
A practical objective is a smooth lower envelope of $\delta E(s)$, supplemented
by penalties for localization, leakage, and control roughness.  The following
sequence avoids conflating distinct questions:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item derive $K(s)$, $M(s)$, and a bound on $\mathcal R(s)$;
 \item diagnose localization using probability profiles, inverse
 participation ratio, or an Agmon/lead-threshold test;
 \item solve the reachability problem before optimizing a schedule;
 \item construct an analytic certificate---weighted Laplacian, corridor
 bracketing, conductance, or a controlled diabatic pair of crossings;
 \item optimize only the remaining amplitudes and switching times;
 \item validate the full Rydberg spectrum and real-time success probability.
\end{enumerate}
Spatially inhomogeneous driving can remove first-order bottlenecks in other
annealing models~\cite{susa2018exponential}, but the projection and certificate
steps are what determine whether that intuition applies to a particular
Rydberg encoding.

\subsection{Limits of the generalization}

For a network of CWE gadgets, the configuration graph is no longer a single
L-shaped waveguide, and a locally preferred corridor need not be globally
consistent.  Selecting a corridor from the known solution would simply hide
the optimization problem in the control.  A scalable construction should
instead use local problem data, a solution-agnostic positive profile, or a
set of controls whose weighted conductance can be bounded for every allowed
logical sector.  Degenerate MWIS instances should be analyzed using the gap
above the desired ground-state manifold rather than an artificial splitting
inside it.

The correct general claim is therefore conditional:
\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.86\textwidth}{
Dynamic local detuning converts an encoding-induced exponential gap to a
polynomial gap when the projected controls can realize a path that removes
every subthreshold localized branch while preserving polynomial
configuration-space conductance and the endpoint cost Hamiltonian.
Time dependence supplies endpoint compatibility and path ordering, but it
does not overcome an instantaneous control-rank obstruction.}}
\end{center}

For the elementary CWE, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mwis-two-atom-controls},
\eqref{eq:mwis-kappa-condition}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:mwis-polynomial-gap} verify these conditions explicitly.  The
same analysis also identifies what remains to be proved before claiming a
general dynamic-encoding advantage: controllability of the projected
potential, a localization-free spectral certificate, and controlled transfer
back to the full Rydberg dynamics.
